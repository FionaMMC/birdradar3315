# BirdRadar
<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform
web

## Product Purpose
Build a recurring connection with real birds through Lights Out: meet a mystery, take a physical after-dark action, watch birds in flight, reveal a bird, earn its stamp, then learn its story. The map and gallery also support independent exploration of historical routes and seasonal observations near home.

## Capabilities and Constraints
Next.js / React / Leaflet, one shared global/nearby map and twelve-month timeline, twenty cited bird profiles, a Lights Out ritual and an earned-stamp gallery. Stamp identity remains concealed during the nightly ritual until an explicit after-dark action confirmation. One immutable surprise assignment and at most one earned record per device-local calendar date; favour species not yet earned. Old bookmarks and quiz flags do not unlock stamps. The gallery shows All / Unlocked / Locked; field notes and maps remain freely accessible. Preserve old journal entries and bookmarked field notes without inventing rewards.

Optional approximate location lets a discovery draw from catalog species actually observed in the selected area in the same calendar month. With no matching sampled records, use an explicitly labelled global discovery. Historical observations and GPS tracks never establish that a bird is above a particular home tonight. Missing data stays missing; independent observations never become connected flight paths.

## Brand Commitments
User further specifies preserving the original modern minimal art style: large background video, soft masks, oversized sans-serif type, and few hard borders. User explicitly requests Apple Weather-like layered heatmaps, translucent milky multicoloured material inspired by IMG_0020.JPG, overlapping species colours and route-density cores, and Balatro-inspired card and foil effects for postage stamps.

## Open Decisions
User confirmed global geography and the current cinematic visual direction after complete mockups. The latest request explicitly changes the collection into a reward for participating in Lights Out, with free exploration alongside it and more real-data species. The existing Vercel site and public GitHub Pages publication are authorized. The audience is curious bird enthusiasts and participants in bird-friendly nights; no account or sensor verification is required.
